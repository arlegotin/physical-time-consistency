The corrected head improves control over the MLP on the complete fixed ten-task direct-torque workload, but it does not improve mean control cost over the generic NODE at the fine internal timestep. This is exploratory evidence from training seed 9000, augmented 20–60 ms data and the shared one-plus-four-step objective. It has no independent-training-seed confidence claim.

Lower cost is better. At 5 ms, the head reduces mean cost by **28.8335% versus the MLP**, with lower cost on all ten tasks. Its mean cost is **0.8974% higher than the generic NODE**, with five wins and five losses. At the nominal 40 ms step, the head's mean cost is 0.9651% below the MLP and 0.6473% below the NODE. Its own mean cost rises 1.5548% under refinement; the MLP's rises 41.3231%, while the NODE's remains identical.

The stricter task-success measure requires both position and velocity tolerances at every physics step during the final 200 ms. The head recorded three nominal successes but no fine-step successes on these ten tasks. The fine-step cost advantage over the MLP therefore does not establish better task success. None of the sixty episodes used the all-candidate-failure fallback.

On the same ten tasks, the saved zero-command mean cost is 4.77768489, and the endpoint-calibrated physics and simulator-oracle controllers both average 0.21044287. The head reduces cost versus doing nothing by 95.59% at 40 ms and 95.52% at 5 ms; its mean cost is respectively 0.11% and 1.67% above those references. Both physical references achieve six of ten task successes, versus three nominal and zero fine-step successes for the head; similar weighted costs do not imply similar strict task success.
